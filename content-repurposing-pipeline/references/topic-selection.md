# Topic And Angle Selection

Use this reference for topic discovery, trend research, angle replacement, and topic ranking. Optimize for qualified attention from the intended reader, not clickbait or unverifiable traffic promises.

First decide whether the article primarily needs timely reach, durable search value, authority, or reader action. The same subject can require a different angle for each outcome.

## Separate The Five Decisions

Do not treat a broad subject as a publishable topic. Define:

- **Subject**: the product, event, problem, or body of material being discussed.
- **Angle**: the specific tension, test, comparison, consequence, or change the article will resolve.
- **Reader promise**: what decision, understanding, or practical advantage the reader gains.
- **Proof mechanism**: the direct evidence, controlled comparison, reproducible test, or visible result that can support the promise.
- **Package**: the title, opening, and lead visual that reveal the same honest promise.

A factual inventory is not yet an angle. Specifications, license terms, model size, architecture, and chronology usually support the article; they should lead it only when their consequence is the reader's actual problem.

## Reconnaissance

Within the authorized source scope, inspect enough of the live conversation to avoid producing another generic overview:

1. Read official material for the exact claim and current capability boundary.
2. Find independent tests, source code, or reproducible demonstrations for observed results.
3. Inspect community questions, failures, cost surprises, and workarounds to discover reader friction. Treat anecdotes as leads, not population-level proof.
4. Check recent coverage and platform conversation to identify saturated framings, unresolved questions, familiar comparison targets, and the useful time window.
5. Separate confirmed facts, plausible explanations, and questions that require an original test.

Stop when new sources repeat known facts without changing the candidate angles. Do not research indefinitely to simulate certainty.

## Generate Candidate Angles

Prefer angles with a concrete consequence and a credible proof path. Useful patterns include:

- **Controlled comparison**: keep the subject and task fixed, change one important variable, and explain a surprising result.
- **Advertised price versus total cost**: compare the headline unit price with the real workflow cost, time, retries, or failure rate.
- **Visible task test**: ask whether the product can complete a recognizable task and show before, during, and after evidence.
- **Claim versus operating boundary**: locate the point where an impressive headline stops helping the intended reader.
- **Common belief versus observed result**: challenge a familiar assumption with evidence rather than contrarian wording alone.
- **New capability versus changed workflow**: show what a reader can now do differently, not merely what a release added.
- **Failure and recovery**: explain a recurring trap, its cause, and a tested way out.

For fast-moving subjects, produce at least two distinct leaders when evidence permits:

- a timely angle that can be published while the conversation is active;
- a durable angle with an original test, useful visual result, or reusable decision framework.

Tag each candidate as `now`, `evergreen`, or `hybrid` so an expiring news hook is not confused with durable reader demand.

## Apply The Attention Gate

Keep a candidate only when most of these questions have concrete answers:

- Who is the reader, and what real decision or frustration do they already have?
- What tension or result makes this article worth opening now?
- Can the central claim be stated in one arguable sentence rather than as a category summary?
- What evidence could prove or disprove it without relying only on a press release?
- Is there a number, comparison, artifact, screenshot, or demonstration that makes the result visible?
- What is different from the most obvious existing coverage?
- Can the promised article be produced honestly within the available time, access, budget, and rights constraints?

Reject or demote a candidate when:

- the title remains valid after swapping in any competing product name;
- it is merely a broad `A versus B` survey with no reader decision;
- its hook requires an unsupported superlative or causal claim;
- all evidence repeats the same announcement;
- the interesting result cannot be demonstrated or sourced;
- the production cost overwhelms the likely reader value;
- caveats erase the promised conclusion rather than define its boundary.

Do not assign decorative scores or predict traffic volume. Use direct comparisons such as `stronger evidence`, `more timely`, `clearer reader payoff`, and `requires original testing`.

## Present The Shortlist

Return three to five candidates at most. For each, state:

- working title or question;
- intended reader and immediate payoff;
- one-sentence central claim;
- why it is timely or differentiated;
- available evidence and the proof still needed;
- lead visual or demonstration opportunity;
- timing as `now`, `evergreen`, or `hybrid`;
- production cost and the limitation most likely to kill the angle.

Lead with the recommendation and explain the tradeoff. Do not bury the strongest candidate in a long undifferentiated list.

If the user asks only to find topics, do not edit article files or workflow state. If the user rejects an existing angle as unfocused, generic, or unlikely to attract attention, reopen topic selection rather than changing only the title or introduction. Rewrite the master only after a replacement angle is selected or an end-to-end request clearly delegates that choice.

## Evidence Boundaries

- Date time-sensitive research and recheck it before drafting or publication.
- A controlled comparison supports conclusions only about the variable changed and the tasks tested. Do not turn an agent-runtime test into a general model-capability ranking.
- Distinguish percentages from percentage points and preserve the original denominator, sample size, task set, and test conditions.
- Treat a community report as an example or research lead unless broader evidence supports prevalence.
- Describe an original test as original only when it was actually run and its artifacts were inspected.
- Surface required accounts, fees, hardware, source access, credentials, or runtime setup as production costs before recommending the angle. If the proof cannot be produced or sourced, demote the angle or record a blocker.

## Generalized Example

For a newly released AI model, `open versus closed, license, and deployment size` is a subject inventory. Stronger angles might ask why the same model produces materially different results in different agent runtimes, or whether a new visual capability can repair a broken interface from screenshots. The stronger versions identify a familiar decision, expose a testable tension, and offer measurable or visible proof. Preserve license and deployment facts as supporting evidence or operating boundaries unless they are themselves the reader's decision.
